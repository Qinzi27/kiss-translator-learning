A little curiosity, every day

A slower way to learn.

Reading in another language is an invitation to notice more. A good translation gives you a place to begin.

When we learn something new, we often look for the fastest route. We collect apps, save articles, and promise ourselves that tomorrow will be different. Yet understanding usually begins with a smaller act: paying attention to one useful idea.

A bilingual page can make that act easier. You can read a paragraph in English, check the Chinese translation, and then return to the original. The goal is to stay curious long enough to understand what the writer is trying to say.

Start with noticing

Choose a short article about something you already enjoy. Gardening, software, cooking, and music all offer familiar ideas in unfamiliar words. Follow an inline link about HTML if you want to explore how web pages are structured. The link should still work after translation.

Before opening a dictionary, ask yourself what you can infer from the surrounding sentences. A new word is easier to remember when it belongs to a concrete situation. Write down the sentence, rather than a lonely definition.

Understanding grows when we give an idea enough space to become familiar.

Translations are useful reading aids. They can also miss tone, context, or a technical meaning. When a sentence seems surprising, compare the original with the translation and look for the source of the difference.

Build a small habit

You do not need a complicated system. A simple routine creates a clear place to start and a gentle reason to return.

  • Read one paragraph without translating it first.
  • Check the translation and choose one expression worth remembering.
  • Explain the main idea in your own words.
  • Return tomorrow and see what you can recall.

In a programming article, expressions such as const message = "Hello, world"; should remain intact. A translation tool should help with the surrounding prose while preserving examples you may want to run.

// This code block must stay unchanged.
const message = "Hello, world!";
console.log(message);

READ IN THE OTHER DIRECTION

换个方向，再读一次

学习一门语言，不只是把一个词换成另一个词。我们也在学习另一种表达习惯：作者先说什么，哪些内容留给读者推断，以及一句简单的话如何传达态度。

试着把这两段中文翻译成英文，再观察译文的句子结构。你可以保留原文、比较措辞，然后用自己的话重新表达。翻译结果适合作为理解和练习的起点。

离线模式适合希望在断网环境中阅读，或希望文本留在本机处理的场景。在线模式可以连接你选择的翻译服务。切换前，先确认当前选择的引擎以及所需的配置。

Make room for questions

At the end of a reading session, write one question that the article left unanswered. A question gives your next session a direction. Over time, these small questions become a map of what you understand and what you still want to explore.

Progress can be quiet. Today you may understand a phrase that stopped you last week. Tomorrow you may notice a subtle difference between two translations. That is enough to keep going.
